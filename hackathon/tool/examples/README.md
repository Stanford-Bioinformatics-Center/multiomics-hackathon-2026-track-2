# Example signatures

Files are generated by `tool/scripts/make_examples.py` (PAH, controls, TCA demo) and `tool/scripts/make_disease_examples.py` (the other disease signatures); `examples.json` lists them all. Columns are `gene_symbol, direction, group, weight, source` (plus `uniprot` where available). `direction` is +1 for up in the disease or condition and -1 for down. `weight` may be empty.

**pah_muscle_malenfant2015.csv** is the test case: the vastus lateralis signature of idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). It contains the 19 proteins that differed between 4 IPAH patients and 4 controls in the iTRAQ proteomics of Malenfant et al. 2015 (J Mol Med, doi:10.1007/s00109-014-1244-0), plus 6 phenotype genes from the same paper's enzyme activity and blot data (CS, TFAM, PDHA1, PDHB down; LDHA, LDHB up). `weight` is the PAH/control ratio; it is empty for the phenotype rows. UniProt accessions come from the MoTrPAC human muscle proteomics feature map. Paper symbols ATP5L and ATP5B appear as ATP5MG and ATP5F1B. The signature has oxidative proteins down and glycolytic and contractile proteins up. Exercise training moves muscle in the opposite direction, so the expected result is that PAH is opposed to the training response.

**pah_blood_cheadle2012_eds.csv** is a blood signature: 22 erythroid genes from the 149-gene erythroid development signature (EDS; Table S2) that Cheadle et al. 2012 reported as up in PAH peripheral blood (GSE33463). All 22 genes are +1. `weight` is the IPAH-vs-healthy limma logFC (30 IPAH vs 41 healthy) from the team's re-analysis of GSE33463. It is intended for probing the MoTrPAC blood data, not muscle.

**hostrup2022_hiit_proteome.csv** is the positive control. It lists the vastus lateralis proteins that changed after 5 weeks of HIIT in 8 men (Hostrup et al. 2022, eLife 11:e69802), filtered at q < 0.05 and deduplicated by gene symbol. `weight` is the post-minus-pre log2 fold change and `uniprot` is the leading accession of the protein group. Because this is a human training signature, it should agree in direction with rat 4 to 8 week muscle protein training responses.

**random_matched.csv** is the negative control. It has 25 random human genes, each drawn from the same decile of human VL RNA AveExpr (EE_vs_CON_24h) as one of the 25 PAH muscle genes, with seed 20260927. Each random gene keeps the direction of the PAH gene it was matched to. APOA1 is not measured in that RNA column, so its match was drawn from the median PAH decile. The score for this set should land near the null median.

**random_mito9.csv** is the pathway-class control. It has 9 random MitoCarta3.0 genes (seed 20260928), all set to -1. They are drawn from genes measured in human VL RNA and PROT at EE_vs_CON_24h and in rat SKM-GN PROT F_8w, excluding the 9 PAH mitochondrial proteins and CS, TFAM, PDHA1 and PDHB. Any set of mitochondrial genes marked "down" is opposed to training, which raises them. This set is therefore expected to look as opposed as the PAH mitochondrial block, which shows how much of the PAH result comes from the pathway class alone.

**tca_intermediates_demo.csv** is a synthetic metabolite demo and not a published signature. It lists 10 metabolites by RefMet name: TCA intermediates, pyruvate and acetylcarnitine (`CAR(2:0)`) at -1, and lactate at +1. The names match the `metabolite_refmet` values in `MotrpacRatTraining6moData::METAB_FEATURE_ID_MAP`; alpha-ketoglutarate is `Oxoglutaric acid`. Its purpose is to exercise the metabolomics path of the tool.

## Published non-PAH disease signatures

The next four files come from `tool/scripts/make_disease_examples.py`, which also writes `examples.json`, the manifest of every example file. The script reads only the cached sources in `examples/sources/` (`RETRIEVAL.tsv` lists the URL, the retrieval date of 2026-09-27 and a checksum for each). The MSigDB cache is written by `tool/scripts/fetch_msigdbr_cgp.R` (msigdbr 26.1.1, MSigDB 2026.1.Hs). None of the gene lists were edited by hand. The only change is that Enrichr's upper-cased open-reading-frame symbols are written in HGNC case (`C2ORF74` becomes `C2orf74`), and any gene listed as both up and down would be dropped (none were). The requested first-choice source was Enrichr's `Disease_Signatures_from_GEO_{up,down}_2014`, and it was not used. Its heart failure sets (GSE3586, GSE5406) and septic shock set (GSE9692) contain none of the textbook markers: no NPPA or NPPB in the HF up sets, and no MMP8, CD177 or S100A12 in the sepsis up set. They also barely overlap MSigDB THUM_SYSTOLIC_HEART_FAILURE. The script prints this check. The crowd-extracted GEO signatures that Enrichr serves as `Disease_Perturbations_from_GEO` and `Aging_Perturbations_from_GEO` (Wang et al. 2016, Nat Commun 7:12846; Enrichr: Kuleshov et al. 2016, NAR 44:W90) pass the check and are used instead. These libraries carry no per-gene weights, so `weight` is empty. The source order is not documented as a ranking, so no set was trimmed to 300 genes.

**type2_diabetes_muscle_mootha2003.csv** is the MSigDB C2:CGP set MOOTHA_VOXPHOS (PMID 12808457): 87 OXPHOS genes, all at -1. Mootha et al. 2003 (Nat Genet 34:267) found by GSEA that this set is coordinately down in the vastus lateralis of men with impaired glucose tolerance or type 2 diabetes, compared with normal glucose tolerance. The file has no up set. None of the sources offers an explicit up/down T2D skeletal muscle signature. The only human T2D muscle entries in `Disease_Perturbations_from_GEO` are cultured myotubes (GSE12643), and they appear twice with up and down swapped, so they were not used. All 87 genes map. The expected reading is that this set looks opposed by training, like the PAH mitochondrial block. Because the set is a pure mitochondrial pathway, compare it with `random_mito9.csv` before reading the result as disease-specific.

**heart_failure_lv_hannenhalli2006.csv** is the Enrichr `Disease_Perturbations_from_GEO_up/_down` entry "Cardiomyopathy C0878544 human GSE5406 sample 88". GSE5406 (Hannenhalli et al. 2006, Circulation 114:1269) compares human left ventricle from ischemic or idiopathic cardiomyopathy with non-failing hearts. Up is higher in failing hearts and down is lower. The file has 291 up and 309 down genes, and 279 up and 292 down map (29 unmapped, mostly LOC and retired symbols such as CTGF). The up set contains NPPA, NPPB, FRZB, ASPN and collagens, and the down set contains SERPINA3 and FCN3. It is intended for the heart layer. The expected reading is mixed. Fetal-gene and ECM/fibrosis genes are unlikely to be opposed by training in healthy rat heart, and any metabolic genes in the set may be.

**aging_muscle_liu2013.csv** is the Enrichr `Aging_Perturbations_from_GEO_up/_down` entry "Human biceps brachii muscles 24 years vs 70 years GDS4858 aging:33". GDS4858/GSE38718 (Liu et al. 2013, J Gerontol A 68:1035) compares biceps brachii from men and women aged 65 to 76 with those aged 19 to 28. Up is higher in old muscle and down is lower. The file has 233 up and 367 down genes, and 200 up and 322 down map (78 unmapped). The up set contains metallothioneins (MT1X, MT2A), which are established markers of muscle aging, plus adipocyte genes (CIDEA, CIDEC, LEP). The Welle vastus lateralis sets (GDS288 and GDS473) were not used because they come from the U133B array only (mostly ESTs) and contain no aging markers. Two other Enrichr entries for GDS4858 (aging:10 and aging:11) overlap this one heavily. The expected reading is weak or partial opposition, because early aging-muscle signatures agree poorly across studies. Metallothionein and adipose genes probably do not track training.

**septic_shock_blood_cvijanovich2008.csv** is the Enrichr `Disease_Perturbations_from_GEO_up/_down` entry "Septic Shock C0036983 human GSE9692 sample 307". GSE9692 (Cvijanovich et al. 2008, Physiol Genomics 34:127) compares whole blood from children with septic shock on day 1 with normal controls. Up is higher in septic shock and down is lower. The file has 494 up and 106 down genes (the up set exceeds 300 and is kept whole, as noted above), and 419 up and 91 down map (90 unmapped). The up set contains neutrophil and granule genes (MMP8, CD177, S100A12, ARG1, OLFM4), and the down set contains NK and T-cell genes (PRF1, NKG7, KLRD1). It is intended for the blood layer. The expected reading is that the score is driven by blood cell composition, with more neutrophils and fewer lymphocytes, rather than by a disease process that exercise could reverse. It will probably not look opposed, or only in proportion to how training shifts leukocyte proportions.

## pah_blood_gse33463_ranked.csv.gz (full disease ranking)

The genome-wide IPAH-minus-healthy PBMC ranking from the team pipeline on main (`MoTrPAC Hackathon/scripts/07_fit_gse33463_blood_limma.R`, output `outputs/PAH blood/pah_blood_gene_ranked.csv.gz`, collection c2.0 run of 26 Sep 2026): GEO GSE33463, 30 idiopathic PAH vs 41 healthy PBMC samples, probe-level limma, one probe per gene (largest |t|), 19,593 genes. Columns used: `gene_symbol`, `t` (the ranking statistic), `logFC`, `adj_p_value`. Because it has a `t` column and no `direction` column, `mprobe run` treats it as a ranking: whole-ranking Spearman association and GO:BP pathway concordance (sections R and P, the team's scripts 08–11 generalised), plus a derived directional signature (BH < 0.05, top 250 per direction) for the other sections. Expected: small positive rank correlations with MoTrPAC whole-blood RNA after exercise (team pipeline: ρ −0.079 and −0.029 during exercise, +0.060 to +0.125 afterwards) and 27 same- / 13 opposite-direction jointly significant GO:BP set × time rows.
